Note: This is a response I wrote to someone who asked a question about how ex-evangelicals view the church's horrific relationship with LGBT+ people. It was in a response to the death of a member of OP's church, who committed suicide after their father disowned them for coming out as gay. I don't have the original post as OP deleted it. In the post, OP asked what the church does that drives queer people to kill themselves and what can be done to fix the problem to make church "safe" for queer people. She proposed some potential responses that her deceased church member's father could have used instead of disowning the kid and asked our opinion on their validity. She also indicated that they were trying to decide if homosexuality was "wrong."

Tl;dr: There isn't a single fix or a simple fix outside of radical acceptance as a bare minimum. The church exists in and perpetuates the culture that drives LGBT+ people to suicide. Attempts at "hate the sin; love the sinner" are hateful and queerphobic, even from well-intentioned people. You can stay a christian and do better, but it will be difficult.

==================

First, I can tell you for sure homosexuality isn't wrong, but I'm guessing just that assertion on its own isn't enough to convince you. I do have a lot of evidence from experience for this, and I don't mean sexually; I'm more talking about the way LGBT+ people show love to their friends. Attending Pride events, for example, certainly makes me think of "they shall know you are [the god of Love's] disciples, if you love one another". This is actually part of my journey away from organized christianity and into a sort of spiritual humanism that seeks to love my fellow man. I was "converted" to the "gospel" of love by queer people the exact way people are supposed to be converted to christianity, but for some reason never are. Pride and LGBT+ solidarity seems to exemplify jesus's teaching *far* better than anything I've ever seen from a church.


**What drives people to suicide?**

Addressing your question more specifically: What drives queer people to suicide? I don't think we know enough to answer this, partially because christians have worked hard to restrict medical understanding and investigation into what's going on in queer people's brains and bodies. There isn't one single factor you can point to in order to say "if we can fix that, the church can be a safe place for queer people." It is the totality of the LGBT+ experience in the current (christian-dominated, western) society that acts together to drive people to suicide. I'll list some examples:

1) From birth, people make fun of you for acting slightly abnormally or liking the "wrong" toys.
2) Your parents act embarrassed or apologize for your behaviour that is slightly different from normal.
3) Every week, you hear an authority figure say "You are evil. Also people who think this way are *extra* evil." You think that way. Therefore you are more evil than everyone else. This is reinforced constantly. Nobody ever questions this, so it must be true, and also there must be nobody else like you.
4) People tell you "I'll love you no matter what." But they have consistently agreed with the authority figure that people like you are the most evil people ever and should be hated. You are therefore *constantly in danger* just by existing, even of someone claims to be your friend.
5) You learn to police every action, making sure that nobody ever sees your true self, because, again, it's evil.
6) Sometimes, you still get caught looking too long at the "wrong" thing. Your parents remember the wrong toys and abnormal behaviours and make fun of you *even though* you try constantly to be good.
7) Puberty hits. Everything is unfamiliar. The authority figure now tells you not only that people like you are evil, but also that urges like you have are evil. You're double extra evil.
8) Many of those urges are stronger than you can handle. Maybe you keep them under control, but they come out as anger. Maybe you don't, and you have no choice to give in to the worst evil imaginable.
9) You now can't even trust people who would let others confess sins to them. If you tell your parents or your pastor, they won't let you have any friends. They might (probably will) disown you or threaten you.
10) Your entire life up until this point has been spent experiencing only fear, self-hate, anger, and dissatisfaction. Nobody has ever loved you entirely for yourself. You have *never felt peace.* 

Which number above is the thing that drove our example person to suicide? Obviously not a single event on its own. The problem is that *the church affirms and facilitates this treatment.*


**Weaknesses of attempting to water down homophobia instead of rejecting it outright**

Looking at some of your specifics: Your specific "corrections," while they appear valid on the surface to people in the christian bubble, miss the entire point. You *cannot* reject a person's sexuality and accept or love them as a person.

>What if she had simply rejected his sexuality, but not him?

This is impossible. We are human. Humans have a sexual drive. Many or all of our sexual preferences are deeply innate. That person's sexuality *is part of them.* That's what we mean when we talk about queer identity. If I reject a person's skin colour but not the person themself, am I *actually* accepting that person? Obviously not!

>What if she had ‘lovingly’ told him that while she disapproved of his sexuality, she still loved him?

How? *How* can you lovingly tell someone, "I find the deepest physical part of you to be evil and reprehensible, literally an affront to morality, but I love you"? Christians like to pretend they can do this, but *thinking this is possible* is part of the problem. Looking at a race example again, consider segregation. "We disapprove of your blackness, but we love you as people" is an unfathomably racist take, and it does nothing but affirm the positions of openly racist people. 


**Conclusion**

Please don't take away from this that I think you're a bad, racist, or inconsiderate person. Your question shows that you are trying to understand and love people, but christianity has prevented you from making it "all the way" to understanding the outsiders who have been hurt by it. I have asked similar questions in terms that were equally ignorant or inconsiderate, and many christian friends have done the same. *You are right to be seeking answers to address this concern, and you are not bad for not knowing the right words.* But it isn't enough yet. Keep searching, and, specifically *make queer friends.* Ask them these questions, let them speak, apologize when you hurt them, and try to improve. 
You don't necessarily have to abandon your christianity to help queer people. I do actually believe christianity can be practiced in a way that doesn't harm queer people. It will require rejecting core tenets of the religion you have been brought up to believe; it will require listening to and living with the ultimate outsiders, people your church sees as deeply sick and painful; it will require your own spiritual death and resurrection. It will require you to place love at the centre of your beliefs and build around that. Modern christianity claims to do that, but it colossally fails, as you've noted in your example. The thing christianity purports to be affirms those difficult tasks, but they'll be "wrong" to your religious intuition and to the christians around you. Fortunately for you, the chief deity of your religion claims to forgive everyone, love everyone, and keep his followers safe from spiritual death. He also taught that when "good" stuff has bad results, it's bad, even if it's dogmatically true, and vice versa; and he also taught that love for your fellow humans is the ultimate form of religious worship. Is it easier to wring your hands while passively tolerating the abuse of LGBT+ people to keep your dogma safe, or do you believe that your chief deity wants you to do good?
