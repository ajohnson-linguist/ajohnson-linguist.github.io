Is it worth it to try to repair the relationship between Indigenous peoples and the church, or is it a lost cause? I'm not sure.  My hottest take is that I think Christianity as it's practiced in the West is a lost cause on its own, so even if the relationship could be repaired, I don't think it should.

I used to think the gospel was infinitely translatable, but there's a language it can't be translated into, and Jesus identifies it: power (i.e. wealth). Christianity only works when its people are the low power group, and Christians, especially white Christians, are the high power group in the West. More than that, a white person going into an Indigenous setting, christian or not, is in power. In that situation, the gospel is tainted and hindered from the start. That's how and why you end up with church-run death camps for kids that stay in operation until 2002. 

The "good" news is that western Christianity is poisoning itself and beginning to rot from the inside. Evangelical support for Trump and blind support for Israel's increasingly glaring genocide is a great example, but that's only revealing the poison that's been in its bloodstream and transmitted via communion to all its members since the 70s and even as far back as the 1800s, 1600s, the Reformation, the Crusades, even back to the first and second centuries with the destruction of so-called heretics.

I don't know what the answer is, but I know that death and resurrection to reject power and destroy sin is a core tenet of the gospel. The church needs to die and a radical rejection of power needs to resurrect in its place. I don't know what that looks like exactly, but I'm beginning to realize that people who do good for its own sake without the christian name or even a concept of Jesus are the "true" christians (to adopt evangelical parlance). A militant atheist card-carrying member of the Satanic Temple who doxxes anti-abortion protesters to protect mothers and burns churches to protest residential schools is absolutely more "saved" than a powerful attendee of a doctrinally-sound church who knows Jesus and speaks in tongues but is hesitant to give a homeless person money in case they use it on drugs.

So my stance now is as follows:

Let missions be done atheistically and humanistically. Instead of the gospel, share food. Instead of learning sound doctrine, learn to sin ethically. Teach folk tales and parables instead of truth. Hold only facts sacred, and, above all else, be wrong. Reject the right; hold only to the good. And, finally, enact the good. True religion has no god because it killed god. It killed its own god by killing itself on behalf of the Other, and it gave its power away to the powerless. If power returns to it, it must be killed again.
