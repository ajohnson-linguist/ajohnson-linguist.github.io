As Independence Day nears, I want to draw attention to an often overlooked component of America’s history: the countless diverse peoples on whose genocides our nation is founded. We can divide these peoples into two major groups, enslaved Africans and displaced Indigenous Turtle Islanders. 

Fortunately, the domestic portion of the African genocide has largely ended. There is of course tremendous work to be done to completely enfranchise the descendants of the people we kidnaped, enslaved, bought, and sold, but Juneteenth is a national holiday now, so everything’s pretty much fixed, right? We fought a war, started some new denominations of churches, murdered protesters, assassinated peaceful figureheads, and re-enslaved people through the prison industrial complex if they survived encounters with law enforcement. All good now. That was centuries ago. Well, decades. Or maybe it’s still going on. Who can say?

There is another major American genocide that has lasted longer and is still in effect in greater force, and yet Americans in general think about it much less often. Indigenous Americans were also forcibly displaced, wantonly killed, and deceived by American leadership. When I say that sentence, your mind probably went to the 1700s or the 1800s, maybe even the early 1900s, but I am referring to the present. America only admitted recently (the 1980s) to blatantly breaking countless treaties made with Indigenous peoples, and despite legal acknowledgement of their blatant flouting of the law, they have taken no tangible actions to begin making anything close to right. Residential schools – complete with hidden mass graves for raped and murdered children – remained in operation until the 2000s. Leonard Peltier is still, right now, in federal prison for a crime he certainly did not commit, and he is continually being denied parole despite being innocent entirely because he is an Indigenous person who dared speak out against the injustice that the American Empire enacted against his people.

This genocide is not in the past. Some colonizer countries, like Canada, have made efforts to address their violent and disgraceful history. Far too little, much too late, of course, but these are steps to right horrific wrongs. American has done nothing; indeed, it continues to perpetuate its slow, unstoppable, divinely mandated, and manifestly destined genocide of its Indigenous peoples. It continues to hide the suffering of its native peoples on reservations built on stolen, arid land and oh-so-generously carved out and gifted to the people it displaced. These displaced peoples were not the same nation as the land's original inhabitants, but America could not distinguish the peoples from each other, with their high cheekbones and darker skin. Cram them into a desert, kill their animals, and hope they all die of smallpox or alcoholism sooner rather than later.

And, for that matter, while we’re on the subject of America’s genocides, let’s go ahead and address the one America is currently bankrolling in full-force: the genocide in Palestine. The US has singlehandedly, against the conscience of the entire rest of the world, supported a fascist and colonialist campaign, drawn out over decades (and occasionally falsely pretending to neutrality), to wipe out or displace Indigenous Palestinians in the name of Zionism. Ironically, Zionism has its roots in European antisemitism, with its major impetus being that we can take all those nasty Jews and put them “over there" (while also colonizing and obliterating the culture of Mizrahi Jews in the process). These racist overtones have died down, but the associated violence has not: For example, American evangelicals back this genocide in hopes that it will bring about the end of the world. Once Israel has its land back, Jesus will return (and kill all the nonbelievers, including those nasty unbelieving Jews that took back the Holy Land) before making everything perfect for the Christians. Israel itself affirms violence, denying Palestinians even the right to collect rainwater for drinking and farming, all while calling any pushback against their inhumane policies as antisemitic, including when their opponents are themselves of Jewish descent. The degree to which citizens of the American Empire have accepted Israeli propaganda hook, line, and sinker, to the extent of denying or ignoring Palestinians’ humanity (try deflecting this criticism by asking "what about Hamas" again, I dare you), hurts me deeply, in no small part because I believed it for a long time as well.

With all that said, I have found it especially difficult to be celebratory this year. I have plenty of problems with America as it is, and living in Canada has only made the banal horrific realities of living in America appear in starker contrast, but this year is worse. Canada has attempted to address its past evils on Canada Day in past years, mourning its murdered Indigenous children instead of celebrating; America has only gotten worse. If you can celebrate the liberation of a country that rose to power on the back of not one but two overarching categories of ethnic cleansings, barely managed not to back the Nazis by what was essentially sheer luck, and then turned right back around to carry out many, many more, with one currently being sponsored by American leadership, then I suppose more power to you… that power certainly can’t be said to belong to the Indigenous Turtle Islanders; so maybe it’s better used to light fireworks and grill burgers in celebration of Imperial might.
